How a Project for Small Flats Runs

  1. Consultation: we discuss your family, routine, storage needs, style and budget.
  2. Measurement: an accurate survey records every wall, window, beam and service point.
  3. Space planning: furniture layouts are tested to keep circulation clear and storage generous.
  4. 3D visualisation: photorealistic views show how each room will look before anything is built.
  5. Drawings and materials: detailed drawings for carpenters and electricians, plus final material choices.
  6. Execution: supervised work on site to a fixed timeline, with updates at every milestone.

Smart Storage Ideas for Small Flats

  • Loft storage: high cabinets above wardrobes and doors for seasonal items.
  • Under-bed storage: hydraulic beds that hold bedding, luggage and bulky items.
  • Under-stair and niche storage: recessed shelves built into wall thickness.
  • Pull-out units: kitchen pull-outs, shoe racks and laundry baskets that slide away.
  • Multi-purpose furniture: sofa-cum-beds, extendable dining tables and wall-mounted desks.

Choosing Materials for Small Flats

Light-coloured laminates, matte finishes that hide fingerprints, mirrors and glass all help small flats feel bigger and brighter. In kitchens and bathrooms, moisture-resistant boards, quartz counters and good-quality hardware make sure the interiors last. Choosing durable materials is especially important in small flats, because every surface is used every day.

Budgeting Wisely

In small flats, the biggest returns usually come from built-in storage, a well-planned kitchen and good lighting. Decorative extras such as art, rugs and accent lighting can be added gradually over time. A good designer helps you spend first on the things that make daily life easier, and keeps the overall look consistent so later additions fit in naturally without the home ever looking unfinished.
