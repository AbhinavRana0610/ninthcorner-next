What an Interior Designer for Office Projects Actually Does

An office has to support very different kinds of work in one place: quiet focus, quick conversations, formal meetings and video calls. It also has to welcome clients, hold equipment and records, and reflect your brand. An experienced interior designer for office projects balances all of these needs within a fixed area, a fixed budget and, usually, a fixed moving date.

Understanding how your teams work

We begin by talking to the people who will use the space. How many work at desks each day? Which teams need to sit together? How often do meetings happen, and how many people attend? These answers decide the ratio of open workstations to cabins, meeting rooms, phone booths and collaboration areas.

Space planning and circulation

Good planning keeps walking routes short and logical, places noisy functions away from quiet ones, and leaves room for the team to grow. We test layouts on plan and in 3D before anything is built.

Acoustics, lighting and comfort

Noise and poor light are the most common complaints in any workplace. We specify acoustic ceilings, wall panels and soft finishes where they are needed, plan task and ambient lighting to reduce glare on screens, and select ergonomic furniture that supports long working days.

Power, data and services

Floor boxes, cable trays, server or network rooms, air conditioning zones and pantry services are coordinated in the drawings, so the finished office works from day one without trailing cables or overloaded points.

Designing for hybrid work

Many teams no longer come to the office every day. A thoughtful interior designer for office projects plans for this shift with fewer fixed desks, more bookable meeting rooms, video-call booths and shared tables that work for whoever is in that day. The result is a smaller, better-used office that still feels busy and welcoming.

Types of Offices We Design

  • Corporate offices: structured layouts with cabins, boardrooms and reception areas that reflect your culture.
  • Start-up and tech offices: flexible floors with collaboration zones, focus pods and room to expand.
  • Co-working spaces: hot desks, private cabins and shared amenities designed for high occupancy.
  • Professional practices: client-facing offices for consultants, clinics and studios where first impressions matter.

Choosing an Interior Designer for Office Fit-Outs

When comparing firms, ask any interior designer for office work to show completed workplaces, not just renders. Ask who will supervise the site and how the project will be phased if you need to keep working during the fit-out. Check that pricing is broken into milestones. Frequently Asked Questions

Can the office stay open during the fit-out?

In many cases, yes. An experienced interior designer for office refurbishments can phase the work floor by floor or zone by zone so your team keeps working.

When should we hire an interior designer for office space?

Ideally before you finalise the lease, so the designer can confirm that the floor plate suits your headcount and growth plans.
