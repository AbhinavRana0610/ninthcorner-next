What the Best Salon Interior Designer Plans First

A salon is a business that clients sit inside for an hour at a time, so every design decision shows up both on the balance sheet and in the client's mood. The best salon interior designer works through these decisions in a deliberate order, starting with how the business actually runs and only then moving on to colour and décor.

Chair count and spacing

The number of styling stations decides how much revenue the floor can generate, but cramming in too many makes the space feel rushed. We test station counts on plan so stylists have room to move around the chair and clients never feel boxed in. It is the first calculation the best salon interior designer makes.

The client journey

We map the route a client takes: reception, waiting area, consultation, wash, styling, payment and exit. A good plan keeps these routes short and avoids clients with wet hair crossing paths with new arrivals.

Mirror lighting

Lighting at the mirror is the single most important detail in a salon. Even, flattering light on the face helps stylists see true colour and helps clients like what they see. We layer this task lighting with softer ambient light elsewhere.

Wash stations and plumbing

Basins need water, drainage and hot water supply in the right place. Planning these early avoids costly changes to the floor later and keeps the wash area comfortable and private.

Storage and back-of-house

Products, towels, colour mixing and staff areas all need space. When back-of-house is well planned, the front of house stays calm and uncluttered.

Materials That Survive Daily Salon Use

Salons face water, chemicals, heat and constant foot traffic. The best salon interior designer chooses surfaces that look good on opening day and still look good after years of use: water-resistant flooring, easy-to-clean counters, durable upholstery and finishes that do not stain from colour products.

Ideally before you sign the lease. The best salon interior designer can tell you whether a unit can hold the number of stations, wash points and storage your business plan needs.

Can you redesign a salon that is already running?

Yes. Renovations can be phased to limit downtime, and the plan is built around the days and hours your salon cannot afford to close.
