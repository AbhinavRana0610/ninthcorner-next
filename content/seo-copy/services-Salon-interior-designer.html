What a Salon Interior Designer Plans For

Clients judge a salon long before the first cut or treatment. The entrance, the smell, the lighting, the comfort of the chair and the view in the mirror all shape their opinion. Behind the scenes, staff need short walking routes, tidy storage and stations that let them work without strain. A skilled salon interior designer brings these two sides together so the space looks beautiful and runs smoothly.

Reception and waiting

The reception desk sets the tone and handles bookings, payments and retail sales. We design it to be welcoming from the door, with a comfortable waiting area that keeps arriving clients separate from those already in service.

Styling stations

Station spacing, mirror size, chair type and the position of power points for dryers and straighteners all affect how fast and comfortably stylists can work. We plan each station as a small workplace, not just a mirror on a wall.

Wash and treatment areas

Backwash units need plumbing, drainage, hot water and a calm setting. Treatment rooms for skin, nails or spa services need privacy, ventilation and soft lighting. These are planned early so the services fit the plan rather than the other way around.

Lighting

Accurate, flattering light at the mirror is essential for colour work and for client satisfaction. A good salon interior designer layers this task lighting with warmer ambient and accent lighting to create the right mood across the floor.

Durable, Easy-Clean Finishes

Hair, water, colour products and constant use test every surface. We choose flooring, counters, upholstery and wall finishes that resist stains and moisture and are quick to clean between clients, so the salon still looks fresh years after opening.

Planning Around Your Business Model

Every salon makes money differently. Some rely on a high volume of quick services, others on long colour and treatment appointments, and others on retail product sales. Before drawing anything, a good salon interior designer asks how your revenue is made, how many staff work each shift and which services you plan to add. A salon built on quick services needs more stations and a fast flow, while a premium spa needs fewer, larger rooms and more privacy. Getting this right at the start means the space supports your business plan instead of limiting it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I prepare before meeting a salon interior designer?

The site layout or lease plan, your target number of stations and treatment rooms, your services menu and a rough budget.

Can a salon interior designer help with branding?

Yes. Colour, materials, signage and lighting all express your brand, and we design them to work together.

Can my salon stay open during a renovation?

Often, yes. Work can be phased so part of the salon keeps serving clients while other areas are refitted, starting with the zones that matter most.
